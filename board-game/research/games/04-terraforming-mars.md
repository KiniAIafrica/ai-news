# EXHAUSTIVE EXTRACTION: TERRAFORMING MARS

> Condensed-but-complete capture (all 14 sections). The flagship **engine-builder** with **project cards**, **resource conversion**, and a **global/environmental track** — extremely relevant to our build-pipeline + environmental-consequence design.

## 1. Snapshot
- **Designer:** Jacob Fryxelius · **Publisher:** FryxGames / Stronghold · **Year:** 2016
- **Players:** 1–5 (optimal 3–4) · **Age:** 14+ · **Time:** 90–120+ min · **Weight:** 3.26/5 (moderate-heavy)
- **Acclaim:** Deutscher Spiele Preis 2017; peaked BGG #3 (2019–20); modern engine-building flagship. Praised for replayability/theme; criticised for length, rules density, downtime, AP.

## 2. Core fantasy
Competing **megacorporations terraforming Mars** — raise oxygen (plants→greenery), temperature (energy→heat), and oceans, building an economic engine of project cards.

## 3. Goal & win/lose
- **Game ends** when all three global parameters are maxed (oxygen 14%, temp +8°C, 9 oceans).
- **Win:** most **Victory Points** = Terraform Rating (rises with every parameter step) + milestones (5 VP, max 3) + awards + map tiles + card VP. Tie-break: most MC.

## 4. Full rules & turn structure — "generations"
1. **Turn order.** 2. **Research:** simultaneously draw 4 cards, keep at **3 MC each**. 3. **Action phase:** 1–2 actions/turn until all pass — play a project card (pay cost, resolve, gain tags), use a card action, do a **standard project** (power plant/asteroid/aquifer/greenery/city), claim a **milestone** (8 MC), fund an **award**, or convert 8 heat→temp / 8 plants→greenery. 4. **Production:** energy→heat; gain resources = production track (+ TR for MC). Repeat.

## 5. Economic / resource systems
- **Six resources:** MegaCredits, steel (discounts building cards), titanium (discounts space cards), plants, energy (converts to heat each gen — "spend or lose"), heat.
- **Production tracks** generate resources each generation → the engine.
- **Card economy:** ~208 base cards; buy at 3 MC; build a **tableau** with **tags** that combo. Cards are events (one-shot), automated (passive), or active (usable).

## 6. Decision dynamics
- **The key tension: engine (production) vs points.** Engine cards give little VP; VP cards give little production. Ramp early but don't end point-poor.
- **Card-draw variance** (mitigated by optional **drafting** variant).
- Milestone/award **races**, map adjacency, corporation choice.
- **Light, mostly parallel interaction** (by design); high skill over high luck.

## 7. Progression / growth — the engine-building heart
Gen 1–3 subsistence → 4–8 ramp → 9–14 explosion (each resource feeds the next: energy→heat→temp→+TR→more MC→better cards→more production). The **snowball** is the core satisfaction; delayed gratification + emergent synergies + scaling.

## 8. Tension, drama & fun
"Turning the corner" when the engine ignites; late-game suspense as the three tracks fill and the end looms; milestone/award races; end-game scoring flips (a point-focused player beats a bigger engine).

## 9. Replayability
**Massive card pool** (500+ with expansions) + 12 asymmetric corporations + drafting variant + 5 modular expansions (Prelude, Colonies, Venus, Turmoil, new maps) + solo mode. Every game's available cards differ.

## 10. Components & physical design
Mars board, player boards (6 resources + production), cubes, tiles (greenery/city/ocean), 12 corporations, ~200 project cards, rulebook. **Infamously mediocre component quality** (thin cards, fiddly cubes) — many upgrade. Steep learning curve; dense small card text.

## 11. Accessibility & scaling
Solo–5p; 3–4 optimal. 14+. **Heavy downtime + analysis paralysis**, especially at 5p. Solo mode great for learning.

## 12. Criticisms & weaknesses
Long; rules-dense; card luck; AP; limited direct interaction; big table footprint; fiddly bits; error-prone end scoring; possible runaway leader; some card imbalance.

## 13. Transferable design principles

**Emulate (high value for us)**
1. **Engine-building gamefeel** — weak early → explosive late; "build an unstoppable research machine" maps perfectly to scaling an AI org.
2. **Project cards as the content/replayability engine** — 150–250 thematic AI cards (data/compute/power/talent/pretraining/fine-tuning/testing/market), shuffled each game → near-infinite variety. *This pairs perfectly with the founder's "infinite model-card" goal.*
3. **Multi-resource conversion as the decision hub** — MC + talent + data + compute + power + heat (+ alignment), with conversions (data+compute→model, model→revenue).
4. **A global/environmental track** — compute/power usage fills an **environmental (Heat) track**; high usage → penalties; sustainable cards (solar/efficiency) score back. Directly delivers the founder's environmental-consequence layer.
5. **Multiple scoring vectors** — research impact + market deployments + breakthroughs (milestones) + recognition (awards) + publications (card VP) → many paths to win, comebacks possible.
6. **Tag-based synergies** — Data/Compute/Talent/Safety/Speed/Ethics tags reward focused "engine" identities.
7. **Asymmetric starting orgs** (corporations) — different labs (data-focused, compute-focused, safety-focused…) → varied strategies.
8. **Optional draft variant** for advanced players (more interaction/skill).
9. **Plan expansions early** (governance, multimodal, safety, open-source) — base game complete, expansions add mechanics not just cards.

**Avoid**
- **Length & analysis paralysis** → simpler math, turn timers, Prelude-style fast start.
- **Too little interaction** → add positional competition (limited market/deployment slots, talent/data races).
- **Component overload / fiddliness**; **error-prone scoring** → streamline.
- **Runaway leader** → competitive milestones/awards + catch-up.
