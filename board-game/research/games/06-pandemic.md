# EXHAUSTIVE EXTRACTION: PANDEMIC (+ PANDEMIC LEGACY)

> Condensed-but-complete capture (all 14 sections). The benchmark **cooperative** game (for our team/classroom mode) and, via **Legacy**, the model for an **updatable, evolving campaign**.

## 1. Snapshot
- **Designer:** Matt Leacock (Legacy with Rob Daviau) · **Publisher:** Z-Man · **Year:** 2008 (Legacy S1: 2015)
- **Players:** 2–4 (6 w/ expansions) · **Age:** 8+ (Legacy 13+) · **Time:** 45–60 min (Legacy 12–24 sessions) · **Weight:** ~2.3–2.5
- **Acclaim:** defined modern co-op gaming; **Pandemic Legacy S1** was BGG #1 for ~2 years (invented the legacy-campaign subgenre).

## 2. Core fantasy
A team of **disease-fighting specialists** racing to cure four global diseases before the world is overwhelmed — **shared heroism under escalating pressure.** Legacy adds an evolving conspiracy narrative with permanent consequences.

## 3. Goal & win/lose
- **Win (co-op):** discover all 4 cures. **Lose (3 ways):** 8 outbreaks · a disease's cubes run out · the player deck empties. Managing **three simultaneous crises** (spatial, material, temporal) is the design.

## 4. Full rules & turn structure
Per turn: **4 actions** (move / treat / build research station / share knowledge / discover a cure with 5 same-color cards) → **draw 2 player cards** (Epidemic cards raise infection rate, infect a new city, and **reshuffle the discard back on top**) → **infect** cities per the infection rate; 4th cube → **outbreak** (chain reactions). **7 asymmetric roles** (Medic, Scientist, Dispatcher, Quarantine Specialist, etc.). **Difficulty knob:** 4/5/6 Epidemic cards.

## 5. Economic / resource systems — a "triple-constraint" economy
- **Actions** (4/turn) = the primary currency. **Cards** (enable actions + needed to cure; 7-card hand limit; Epidemics are punishments in the deck). **Disease cubes** (finite; running out = loss). **Research stations** (max 6). **Infection rate** (rises, never falls). Every choice trades one scarcity for another.

## 6. Decision dynamics
- **Cooperative, open-information** decision-making; **role synergy** drives interdependence.
- **Escalating, self-tightening tension** (no GM needed).
- **The alpha-player ("quarterback") problem** — one dominant player can solve everyone's turn; mitigated (not solved) by asymmetric roles + table culture.

## 7. Progression / growth
- **Within a game:** research-station network; each cure permanently powers down a threat.
- **Legacy (the breakthrough):** a **12-month campaign** with sealed packets, **stickers**, **permanently destroyed cards**, evolving rules, character progression, and branching — 12–24 sessions of a single, group-unique story.

## 8. Tension, drama & fun
The dreaded **infect step**, near-misses, cascading outbreaks, and **shared victory/defeat** (no blame-shifting). Legacy adds narrative momentum and emotional investment.

## 9. Replayability
Base: variable setup, role combos, difficulty knobs, expansions. **Legacy:** new rules every month sustain a long campaign — but it's **one-and-done** (designed to be consumed); replay via Season 2 / new copy. The **legacy/campaign model is the key lesson for an *updatable* product.**

## 10. Components & physical design
World map (48 cities), role cards + pawns, 96 disease cubes, infection + player decks, markers, ~15-page rulebook. Legacy adds sealed packets, sticker sheets, hidden components. Teach ~15–20 min; medium complexity; clear color-coding.

## 11. Accessibility & scaling
2–4 (3–4 sweet spot); threat is **external** so it scales well with appropriate difficulty. Solid **solo** mode (removes the alpha-player issue). 8+ (Legacy 13+).

## 12. Criticisms & weaknesses
**Alpha-player/quarterbacking**; can feel **solvable/scripted** after many plays; repetitive without expansions; occasional unwinnable bad-luck curves; hand-limit punishment; Legacy rules-overhead.

## 13. Transferable design principles

**Emulate**
1. **A real CO-OP / TEAM mode** (great for classrooms/families) — shared goal (build the model to spec), multiple loss conditions (bad outputs / insufficient training / compute depleted).
2. **Role specialization & asymmetry** — Data Engineer, Model Architect, Validator (prevents "overfitting outbreaks"), Deployer — each essential, reducing alpha-player dominance.
3. **Built-in escalating threat** — a complexity/overfitting/compute-cost meter that self-tightens (no GM needed).
4. **Triple-constraint economy** — actions + data/cards + compute "cubes" + infrastructure, interdependent.
5. **Simple difficulty knobs** set at setup (3/4/5/6 "challenge" cards; dataset size; adversarial mode).
6. **The LEGACY/CAMPAIGN model for an updatable game** — a multi-session "season" with unlockable packets and persistent changes; *perfect* for teaching that AI development is iterative, and for shipping ongoing content updates (matches the founder's "pushed updates / never outdated" vision).
7. **Shared narrative & emotional weight** (the model serves a critical real use; failure has consequences).
8. **Mandatory discussion/table-talk** (great for classroom learning).

**Avoid / design against**
- **Alpha-player dominance** → give roles *qualitatively different information/domains*, not just numeric perks.
- **Solvable/scripted feel** → escalation + variability + campaign content.
- **Kingmaker/sabotage** in co-op → roles enable, don't veto.
- **Analysis paralysis** → turn timers, clear role "domains," threshold rules.
- **Education overshadowing fun** → game first, lesson emerges naturally.
