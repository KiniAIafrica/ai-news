# MECHANIC STUDY: POKÉMON TRADING CARD GAME (TCG)

> Condensed-but-complete capture. Studied (at the founder's request) for **collection, "powering up," deck/loadout building, and near-infinite replayability via expansions** — to inform our **Model-Card** system and content strategy. (Note: includes an important ethical flag on gambling-style mechanics for an educational product.)

## 1. Snapshot
- **Publisher:** The Pokémon Company (orig. WotC) · **Year:** 1996 (JP), 1999 (EN). 30-year run; ~10.2B cards printed in 2024; FY2025 ≈ $2.9B net sales (+38% YoY). Mature competitive circuit (Worlds since 2004); huge secondary/graded market (~$10B).
- Endures via iconic IP, a **3-month expansion treadmill**, low competitive entry cost (~$79 deck), collectibility-meets-play, and a **rotation** system that controls power creep.

## 2. Core fantasy
A trainer **building a 60-card deck**, then dueling 1v1 — Pokémon **evolve**, Energy fuels attacks, Trainer cards create synergies, racing to take all Prize cards.

## 3. Goal & win/lose
Three win paths: take all **6 Prize cards** (by KOing opponent Pokémon), opponent has **no Pokémon in play**, or opponent **can't draw** (deck-out). Powerful Pokémon (ex/V/VMAX) are worth **2–3 Prizes** when KO'd → risk/reward.

## 4. Full rules & turn structure
**Deckbuilding:** exactly 60 cards, **max 4 copies** of any card (basic Energy unlimited). **Turn:** draw 1 → main phase (play Basics, **evolve**, **attach 1 Energy/turn**, play Items freely + **one Supporter/turn**, use Abilities, retreat) → **attack** (one). Damage modified by **Weakness (×2) / Resistance**. Evolution chains: Basic → Stage 1 → Stage 2 (can't evolve the turn a Pokémon enters play).

## 5. Resource & "engine" systems
- **Energy attachment (1/turn)** = the core tempo gate; **Energy acceleration** cards are the scarcest, most powerful resource.
- **Evolution = in-game powering up** (weak Basic → strong Stage 2), with a time/consistency cost (**Rare Candy** skips a stage).
- The **deck itself is the built engine** (attacker + draw/search Supporters + Abilities + disruption).

## 6. Decision dynamics
- **Deckbuilding is the meta-game** (which attacker, draw engine, search, energy count, tech cards).
- In-game: sequencing, retreat decisions, Supporter timing, bench management.
- **Luck:** opening-hand bricks, prizing a key card (run 2-ofs not 1-ofs), coin flips. **Skill dominates** over a tournament sample; consistency design (≈20–22 energy, 25–35 trainers, 10–16 Pokémon) reduces variance.

## 7. Progression / growth
- **In-game:** evolution + energy build-up + status removal.
- **Out-of-game (the engagement loop):** pull cards → build/upgrade deck → compete → refine → new set drops → repeat. Plus a collectibility/grading ladder (raw → graded PSA 10).

## 8. Tension, drama & fun
Comeback swings (KO a 2-prize Pokémon twice to catch up), topdecks, big knockouts, disruption (hand-wipe, force-swap), and the **collection thrill / chase cards** (driven by rarity + randomized packs).

## 9. Replayability engine (the key lesson for us)
- **Expansion/set model:** 4 main sets/year + specials → constant new archetypes and meta resets.
- **Rotation** (Standard) retires old cards to fight power creep; **Expanded** preserves legacy.
- Booster-pack randomness + rarity tiers drive engagement — but this is **gambling-adjacent** (see criticisms).

## 10. Components & physical design
Standard CCG-size cards; rarity tiers (common → secret/alt-art/rainbow/gold); booster packs (10 cards, ≥1 rare); starter/theme decks (~$10–15) and kid-friendly "My First Battle" mini-decks for onboarding.

## 11. Accessibility & scaling
Strictly **1v1**; age 7+ official (player base skews adult). Learnable in ~30 min; high skill ceiling. Cost barrier exists; singles market eases budget builds.

## 12. Criticisms & weaknesses (IMPORTANT for an educational product)
- **Gambling-like booster packs** (loot-box psychology; youth vulnerability; Belgium briefly banned packs).
- **Pay-to-win / cost** pressure; **power creep** (mitigated but devalues old cards on rotation); **speculation/investment** culture; variance (bricks, prizing, coin flips).

## 13. Transferable design principles

**Emulate**
1. **A fixed "deck"/loadout constraint** (e.g., a bounded set of attribute cards) forces intentional synergy — adapt to our **Model-Card assembly**.
2. **A "one per turn" resource gate** (like 1 Energy/turn) for clean tempo and to limit runaway advantage.
3. **A copy limit (e.g., 4)** to ensure variety and prevent dominant "super cards."
4. **In-game "powering up"** via stages (Untrained → Intermediate → Advanced model) with time costs and power gains.
5. **The EXPANSION/CONTENT-PACK engine** for near-infinite replayability — *this is the founder's "infinite model-card / pushed-update" vision*; ship periodic content packs, with a **rotation/"classic format"** to manage freshness without obsoleting learners.
6. **Variable "prize weight"** — more powerful models are worth more if they fail (risk/reward).
7. **Multiple viable archetypes** (accuracy / speed / ensemble / regularized) so no single best build.
8. **Matchups as flavor, not hard counters** (~40% matchup / 60% skill).

**Avoid (ethically critical for education)**
1. **Gambling mechanics / randomized paid packs** — if using card packs, **publish odds** and/or use **draft/sealed/curated** distribution; favour fixed, transparent content.
2. **Pay-to-win pricing** — free starter content; monetize cosmetics/components, not competitive power.
3. **Uncontrolled power creep** — keep a **classic format** where older cards stay viable.
4. **Speculation/investment culture** — reward play/learning (rankings/badges), not card scarcity.
5. **Deckbuilding overload** — provide visual templates and progressive complexity.
