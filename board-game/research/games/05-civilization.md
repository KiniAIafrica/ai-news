# EXHAUSTIVE EXTRACTION: CIVILIZATION-BUILDING BOARD GAMES

> Condensed-but-complete capture. Focus on **Through the Ages: A Story of Civilization** (Vlaada Chvátil), with **Sid Meier's Civilization: The Board Game** (FFG 2010) and the original **Civilization** (Francis Tresham, 1980) — the tech-tree/empire-building tradition.

## 1. Snapshot
- **Through the Ages (TtA):** CGE, 2006 (revised "A New Story…" 2015), 1–4p, 14+, 2–4 hrs, weight ~4.4/5, long-time **BGG top 5**. The card-driven civ-building flagship.
- **Sid Meier's Civilization (FFG 2010):** Kevin Wilson, 2–4p, 14+, 3–5 hrs, weight ~3.7. Recognisable video-game civ feel; multiple victory paths.
- **Civilization (1980):** Francis Tresham; **invented the board-game tech tree** that inspired the entire 4X video-game genre; 2–7p, 8–12 hrs.

## 2. Core fantasy
Lead a civilization from antiquity to the modern age — advance technology, build wonders, appoint leaders, and accumulate dominance (cultural, military, scientific, economic).

## 3. Goal & win/lose
- **TtA:** most **culture points** at end of Age III (from production, wonders, techs, leaders, military). No hard loss, but unrest → uprising (skip production).
- **FFG Civ:** four victory paths — **Military** (take a capital), **Science** (Space Flight), **Economic** (15 trade), **Cultural** (culture track). Elimination possible.
- **1980:** furthest up the Advancement Slide Track (civilization cards owned).

## 4. Full rules & turn structure (TtA focus)
Per turn: **replenish the card row** (older cards cost fewer civil actions — 1 on the left, up to 3 on the right) → **production** (food/resources/science/culture) → **action phase** spending fixed **Civil + Military actions** (set by your government) to take cards, grow population, build, research techs, build multi-stage wonders, appoint leaders → **politics** (resolve aggressions/wars) → **check happiness/unrest**. Governments (Despotism→Democracy/Communism) trade military for civil actions.

## 5. Economic / resource systems
- **Four interdependent pillars:** food (feeds population; excess rots), resources/ore (build), science (research techs), culture (victory). Plus **happiness** (too few → uprising) and **military strength**.
- Everything trades off: more farms = less science/military this turn. **No dominant path**; the card row dictates what's available.
- **1980's trade system:** commodity set-collection with **N² payouts** + table negotiation/blocking — a brilliant pure-economics engine.

## 6. Decision dynamics
- **Agonising action economy** (TtA): few actions, many temptations; the **card-row aging** mechanic = visceral opportunity cost (wait for it to cheapen vs grab it before a rival/discard).
- Government choice = long-term path dependency. Military threat is unpredictable (bluffing/reputation).
- Low luck (TtA, card-row randomness only); FFG adds combat-card luck; 1980 adds calamities + heavy negotiation.

## 7. Progression / growth — THE TECH TREE (key takeaway)
- **TtA (soft tree):** cards filtered by Age; any order within an age; **compounding** (early tech/government/wonders pay exponential dividends late).
- **FFG (pyramid):** need ≥N−1 lower-level techs before level N → forces broad development before specialising.
- **1980 (strict prerequisite DAG):** linear branching chains teach concept sequencing.
- Snowball arc: antiquity infrastructure → mid-game ramp → modern-age payoff.

## 8. Tension, drama & fun
The "civilization arc" (rise from antiquity to modernity), agonising every-turn decisions, surprise military threats, end-game culture surge, and (1980) cut-throat trade negotiation. **Sags:** length, complexity, downtime/AP.

## 9. Replayability
Huge card variety (techs/wonders/leaders), variable governments, multiple paths/victory types, expansions. Different card sets favour different strategies each game.

## 10. Components & physical design
Card decks (civil/military), player mats, cubes/tokens, central board (card row). **Heavy:** TtA rulebook ~20–24 pp, steep curve, notable bookkeeping; FFG is more accessible (modular map, plastic units, recognisable theme).

## 11. Accessibility & scaling
TtA 2–3 sweet spot, 14+, **hard to teach** (~30 min), high AP; FFG 3–4, more intuitive; 1980 2–7 but **8–12 hrs** = extreme barrier. TtA offers tiered Simple/Advanced/Full rules.

## 12. Criticisms & weaknesses
Complexity overload; length; runaway leader (FFG/1980 lack strong catch-up; TtA original had balance issues, fixed in 2015); downtime/AP; military-draw luck (TtA); combat-card luck (FFG); calamity luck (1980); fiddliness.

## 13. Transferable design principles

**Emulate**
1. **TECH TREE as the core spine** — Data → Compute → Pretrain → Fine-Tune → Deploy as a prerequisite progression; *within* each stage allow flexible choices (data cleaning vs augmentation). Teaches sequencing AND offers freedom. **A hybrid soft/strict tree fits us best.**
2. **Tight action economy** — limited actions/turn (research vs deploy), visceral trade-offs.
3. **The aging card-row** — a shared market where cards get cheaper over time but may be taken/discarded → real opportunity-cost tension (great for a data/compute marketplace).
4. **Multiple intertwined resources** (data, quality, compute, knowledge, trust, speed) that must be balanced — neglect one and you "collapse" (e.g., ignore testing → trust crash). Cap at ~4–6 types.
5. **Compounding growth** — early infrastructure pays off exponentially → teaches long-term thinking.
6. **Multiple victory paths** (data-first / compute-first / ethical / speed) → replayability + inclusivity.
7. **Catch-up via events** (open-source breakthrough, competitor bug) to curb runaway leaders.
8. **Thematic integration / stealth learning** — name things thematically; mechanics mirror AI reality.

**Avoid**
1. **Overwhelming complexity** — aim FFG-level (10–15 pp rules, ~2–3 hr), NOT Through-the-Ages-level, for an educational/mass product.
2. **Unrecoverable early mistakes** — allow pivoting/catch-up.
3. **Crippling length & downtime/AP**.
4. **Runaway leader without brakes**.
