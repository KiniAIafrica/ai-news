# EXHAUSTIVE EXTRACTION: MONOPOLY

> Condensed-but-complete capture (all 14 sections). Monopoly is the founder's chosen "feel" anchor — property/economy + dice movement + chance cards + trading.

## 1. Snapshot
- **Origin:** Elizabeth Magie's *The Landlord's Game* (patented 1904), designed to teach the evils of land monopoly; commercialised by Charles Darrow → **Parker Brothers, 1935**; now **Hasbro**.
- **Players:** 2–8 (optimal 3–5). **Age:** 8+. **Time:** 1–3+ hrs. **Weight:** low-moderate.
- **Impact:** ~275M+ copies, 47 languages, 114 countries — most-played board game. Endures via nostalgia, social negotiation, endless themed editions, mobile (*Monopoly GO!* hit $6B in 2025).

## 2. Core fantasy
A real-estate tycoon buying property, building houses/hotels, and bankrupting rivals through escalating rent. Monopoly = market domination.

## 3. Goal & win/lose
- **Win:** last player solvent (zero-sum). **Lose:** can't pay a debt → bankrupt, surrender assets, **eliminated permanently** (progressive elimination).

## 4. Full rules & turn structure
Setup: $1,500 each, tokens on GO, shuffle Chance/Community Chest. **Turn:** roll 2 dice → move → resolve the space (buy unowned property *or it goes to auction*; pay rent on owned; draw Chance/Chest; pay tax; Jail). **Doubles** → roll again; three doubles → Jail. **Pass GO** → +$200. **Build** only on a complete color set, **evenly**; 4 houses → hotel. **Mortgage** unimproved property for half value; lift at +10%. **Jail:** pay $50 / "Get Out of Jail Free" / roll doubles. **House/hotel scarcity:** only 32 houses + 12 hotels (a real bottleneck).

## 5. Economic / resource systems
- **Finite money** ($1,500 start, no injection in official rules — Free Parking payout is a house rule that breaks this and lengthens games).
- **28 purchasable properties** (22 streets in 8 color groups, 4 railroads, 2 utilities).
- **Steep rent curves:** monopoly doubles base rent; 3rd house is the ROI inflection; hotels extract max rent (Boardwalk hotel = $2,000).
- **House scarcity** as a weapon — building aggressively starves opponents of materials.
- **Mortgage** = survival tool with a 10% penalty.
- **Trading:** anything for anything, any terms — the social heart of the game.

## 6. Decision dynamics
- Meaningful choices exist (which property, build vs hoard, when to mortgage) but are **heavily constrained by dice/cards** (~60–70% luck).
- **No catch-up mechanics** → strong runaway-leader problem; outcome often decided by mid-game.
- **Kingmaking:** a near-bankrupt player chooses the winner via who they trade with.
- Interaction is **negotiation-heavy but mechanically limited** (you can't directly attack; rent + trading are the levers).

## 7. Progression / growth
Fragmentation → consolidation (trading for monopolies) → development (houses/hotels) → dominance (rent snowball) → elimination. **Positive feedback for the leader, negative spiral for the behind player.**

## 8. Tension, drama & fun
Negotiation theatrics, near-bankruptcy moments, "you landed on my hotel!", monopoly-completion dread, jail reversals. **Sags:** length/downtime, early elimination, predictable runaway leader, luck swings, under-used auction rule, Free-Parking house-rule trap.

## 9. Replayability
High for casual/family (dice + negotiation variability + themed editions + emergent stories); **low for hardcore** (fixed property values, convergent optimal strategy, predictable arcs, long runtime).

## 10. Components & physical design
Board (40 spaces), 8 tokens, 2 dice, 28 title deeds, 32 houses, 12 hotels, 16 Chance + 16 Community Chest, ~$15k play money, rulebook. Iconic, recognisable; teachable in ~15–30 min but **first game 3+ hrs**; frequent rule misapplication.

## 11. Accessibility & scaling
2–8 players (4 ideal). 8+ ages. Significant **downtime** at 6+ (≈15 min between turns). Multigenerational appeal; accessible editions exist.

## 12. Criticisms & weaknesses
Length; **player elimination**; **runaway leader (no catch-up)**; **kingmaking**; **luck dominance**; frequent math/rule errors; the Free-Parking house-rule trap that worsens all of the above.

## 13. Transferable design principles

**Emulate**
- **Finite money + scarcity tension**; positive-feedback growth (monopoly→houses→hotels) models compounding — but bound it.
- **Persistent asset ownership** (data/compute/models/talent as owned, appreciating assets).
- **Meaningful trading negotiation** as the social centerpiece.
- **Scarcity as a weapon** (limited compute/chips like limited houses).
- **Risk/reward tiers** (cheap-fast vs expensive-powerful builds).
- **Clear win/lose + visible progress** (houses on board ≈ visible model progress).
- **Memorable dramatic moments** from big swings.

**Avoid**
- **Excessive length** → tighter turns, clear endpoint.
- **Player elimination** → keep failing players engaged (pivot/merge/comeback) or use degrees of success.
- **Runaway leader without catch-up** → add rubber-banding (Power Grid-style).
- **Luck > skill** → bias ~60% skill.
- **Kingmaking** → trade rules that prevent it.
- **Rule ambiguity / optional house rules in the core**.
- **Static asset values** → make data/compute/model value shift dynamically.
- **Thematic disconnect** → keep mechanics reinforcing the AI-economics lesson (data quality, compute efficiency, training time, environmental cost).
